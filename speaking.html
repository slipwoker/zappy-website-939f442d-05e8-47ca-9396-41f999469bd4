Signature Topics

What Daria Speaks About

Each lecture is a journey through ideas — from the equations that govern galaxies to the sentences that move hearts. These are the themes that define her work on stage, on the page, and in the air.

  1. 01

    The Poetry of Physics

    What happens when a physicist reads a poem — and a poet reads an equation? This lecture explores the unexpected kinship between scientific language and literary form: the rhythm of a formula, the metaphor hidden in a theorem, the beauty of describing the universe in the fewest possible words. Daria draws on her dual life as a physicist and author to reveal that precision and poetry are not opposites — they are collaborators.

    Science & Language

  2. Storytelling and Truth

    Science advances through evidence; literature advances through empathy. But both are, at their core, acts of storytelling. This talk examines how narrative structure shapes the way we understand facts, how stories can illuminate or distort reality, and why the craft of telling a true story is one of the most demanding — and important — skills of our time. A meditation on honesty, imagination, and the ethics of explanation.

    Narrative & Epistemology
    02
  3. 03

    Science in Public Culture

    At a moment when scientific consensus is contested in public squares and social feeds, how does science communicate itself to the world? Daria examines the gap between the laboratory and the living room — how institutions, media, and individual scientists shape (and misshape) public understanding. Drawing on her experience as a podcast host and public intellectual, she offers a frank, nuanced account of what it means to be a scientist in the age of viral misinformation.

    Public Discourse

  4. Curiosity as a Civic Practice

    Curiosity is not merely a personality trait — it is a democratic virtue. This lecture makes the case that asking questions, tolerating uncertainty, and remaining genuinely open to being wrong are not just scientific habits but essential civic ones. Daria argues that the disposition of the scientist — humble, rigorous, endlessly curious — is exactly what public life needs more of, and explores how we might cultivate it across education, media, and community.

    Education & Democracy
    04
  5. 05

    The Craft of Explaining the Invisible

    Dark matter. Quantum entanglement. The structure of spacetime. How do you explain something that cannot be seen, touched, or directly experienced? This masterclass in scientific communication unpacks the tools — analogy, rhythm, image, silence — that make the abstract tangible. Whether you are a researcher, a teacher, a journalist, or simply someone who has ever struggled to explain a complex idea, this talk offers a practical and philosophical guide to making the invisible felt.

    Science Communication
